Method

A learned baseline, with more prompts per batch

At a fixed rollout budget, sampling more responses to each prompt leaves fewer distinct prompts in the batch. POISE estimates a baseline with a small probe over the actor’s internal states, avoiding a separate policy-scale critic.

  1. Sample two responses

    Generate two independent responses to each prompt and collect their hidden states and token entropy.

  2. Use the other response

    Predict each response’s value baseline from the other response’s features, keeping the baseline independent of the response being updated.

  3. Learn alongside the policy

    Use the resulting advantages to update the policy, and fit the ridge probe to rewards from paired rollouts.

Feature extraction and update order

Mean-pool the final prompt and reasoning token states at a fixed layer. Average token entropy separately over all generated tokens, reasoning tokens, and answer tokens.

Compute baselines before fitting on the current batch. Use the fixed advantages in a PPO-style clipped policy update; fit the ridge probe on paired-rollout rewards from current and recent trajectories.

Cross-rollout independence preserves the unbiased REINFORCE baseline term. The policy is optimized with the clipped surrogate objective.

Why prompt diversity matters: theory and controlled experiment

Let B = n × m be the total rollout budget: n prompts with m responses each. For a fixed policy, prompt distribution, and prompt-specific baseline, the batch gradient covariance is

Cov(ĝ) = Σw / B + (m / B) Σb

Σw and Σb are the within-prompt and between-prompt covariance components. The within-prompt term stays fixed, while the between-prompt term scales with m. This isolates rollout allocation with the baseline held fixed. Section 2.2.

Analysis

From value prediction to policy learning

01 Can internal states predict value?

On Qwen3-4B and DAPO-Math, the probe achieves Pearson r = 0.870 and MAE = 0.141, compared with 0.676 and 0.262 for a finetuned Qwen3-4B critic. Both use the same reward-labeled data; the critic reads the prompt alone, while the probe also observes generated trajectory features.

Across five datasets and four backbones, including GPT-OSS-120B, the probe improves MAE in 16 of 20 model–dataset pairs and correlation in 18 of 20. These experiments measure held-out value prediction.

02 Which inputs make the estimate useful?

Reasoning states and entropy are more predictive than prompt states alone. Combining them gives the lowest MAE and highest correlation in this Qwen3-4B ablation; response length is a weaker predictor.

Probe architecture, layer, and pooling ablations

The linear ridge probe matches or exceeds the tested MLPs in Pearson correlation, although some MLPs have lower MAE. Layer and pooling-window ablations show that value prediction is not confined to a single extraction setting. Extraction ablations; probe architectures.

03 Does the estimator help RL training?

In controlled Qwen3-4B math training, full POISE reaches 50.1% Avg@32 across seven benchmarks, versus 40.7% for the same-rollout variant and 47.5% for a prompt-only estimator.

Additional experiments: token-level PPO and instruction following

Comparison with token-level PPO

On the main multi-domain suite, POISE scores 58.10 versus PPO’s 57.24 on Qwen3, and 49.92 versus 49.33 on OLMo. PPO uses a separate full-parameter token-level critic and one rollout per prompt. PPO is stronger in some individual domains. Table 11.

Separate instruction-following training

Instruction following is held out from the main GURU training mixture. In a separate experiment trained on IF-RLVR, POISE achieves the best IFEval and IFBench scores among the compared methods on Qwen3-4B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B. Table 8.

Scope. The experiments use fixed compute budgets; substantially longer training horizons remain untested. POISE predicts sequence-level value. Token-level credit assignment, non-verifiable rewards, and long-horizon agentic tasks are future directions. Limitations.
