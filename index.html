Your Language Model is Its Own Critic

Reinforcement Learning with Value Estimation from Actor’s Internal States

Yunho Choi*, Jongwon Lim*, Woojin Ahn, Minjae Oh, Jeonghoon Shim, Yohan Jo†

Graduate School of Data Science, Seoul National University

*Equal contribution. †Corresponding author.

Accepted to NeurIPS 2026

Paper arXiv Code BibTeX

TL;DR

Policy Optimization with Internal State Value Estimation (POISE) uses the actor's internal states to learn a reliable value baseline with only two rollouts per prompt, leaving room for more distinct prompts in each training batch. This supports stable multi-domain RL without a separate LLM-scale critic. Experiments show higher overall benchmark scores on two backbones and a lower training variance proxy on Qwen3-4B.

What is this paper about?

Stable reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) depends on both the quality of the value baseline and the diversity of prompts in each batch. A baseline estimates the reward expected for a prompt, allowing the policy to learn from how a response performs relative to that expectation. GRPO estimates it by averaging rewards from several responses to the same prompt. Larger groups improve this estimate but, under a fixed rollout budget, leave fewer distinct prompts per batch. That loss of diversity can increase gradient variance, especially across different reasoning domains. PPO instead learns a separate policy-scale critic, adding substantial training and memory cost.

POISE learns the baseline from hidden states and token entropy already computed by the actor. A lightweight probe is updated alongside the policy, and two independent responses provide each other's baselines. This brings internal-state value estimation into policy optimization while retaining high prompt diversity. We evaluate the resulting algorithm through six-domain RL training, alongside analyses of value prediction and the cross-rollout design.

Motivation

Why prompt diversity matters

With the baseline held fixed, allocating the same rollout budget to more distinct prompts reduces the between-prompt component of gradient variance. A controlled experiment finds a larger allocation effect in multi-domain training than in math-only training.

Same rollout budget, different allocation

Let B = n × m be the batch budget: n prompts with m responses each. For a fixed policy and prompt-specific baseline,

Cov(ĝ) = Σw / B + (m / B) Σb

Σw and Σb describe within-prompt and between-prompt gradient covariance. Increasing m leaves the first term unchanged and scales up the second. A useful baseline therefore needs to work with few responses per prompt. Section 2.2.

Preliminary

Can the actor supply its own value estimate?

We first test whether a lightweight probe can predict expected verifier reward from the actor's internal signals. On held-out Qwen3-4B / DAPO-Math prompts, the probe reaches Pearson r = 0.870 and MAE = 0.141, compared with r = 0.676 and MAE = 0.262 for a separately trained Qwen3-4B critic.

Across five datasets and four backbones, including GPT-OSS-120B, the probe improves MAE in 16 of 20 model–dataset pairs and Pearson correlation in 18 of 20. These experiments assess held-out value prediction; the RL results appear below.

Method

Cross-rollout value estimation

Response-dependent features are useful for prediction, but using them as a baseline for that same response can bias the policy gradient. POISE samples two independent responses and computes each one's baseline from the other's features. This preserves the zero-expectation REINFORCE baseline term.

1

Generate two answers

The old policy samples two independent responses for each prompt.

2

Read internal signals

At a fixed layer, mean-pool the final prompt-token and reasoning-token windows separately. Average token entropy separately over all generated, reasoning, and answer tokens.

3

Swap predicted values

For each response, subtract the baseline predicted from the other response from its reward.

4

Update the policy and probe

Compute baselines before fitting on the current batch. Use the fixed advantages in a PPO-style clipped policy update; fit the ridge probe on paired-rollout rewards and recent trajectories.

Results

Higher overall scores across reasoning domains

On the six-domain GURU training corpus, POISE achieves the highest overall benchmark average among the main baselines on both backbones: 1.35 points above DAPO on Qwen3-4B and 1.96 points on OLMo-3-7B-Instruct-DPO.

Training stability and online value estimation

On Qwen3-4B, POISE has a lower Adam-moment variance proxy over most of training, while its probe remains predictive as the policy changes.

Additional RL comparisons: PPO and instruction following

Comparison with token-level PPO

On the main multi-domain suite, POISE scores 58.10 versus PPO's 57.24 on Qwen3, and 49.92 versus 49.33 on OLMo. PPO uses a separate full-parameter token-level critic, GAE, and one rollout per prompt. PPO is stronger on some individual benchmarks. Table 11.

Instruction-following training

Instruction following is held out from the main GURU training mixture. In a separate experiment trained on IF-RLVR, POISE achieves the best IFEval and IFBench scores among the compared methods on Qwen3-4B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B. Table 8.

Analysis

Which design choices matter?

Two ablations examine different parts of POISE: which signals help predict value, and how those signals should enter the policy update.

Input features: reasoning states and entropy improve prediction

On Qwen3-4B, reasoning states and entropy are more predictive than prompt states alone. Combining all three gives the lowest MAE and highest correlation in this ablation; response length is a weaker predictor.

Probe architecture and feature extraction

The linear ridge probe matches or exceeds the tested MLPs in Pearson correlation, although some MLPs have lower MAE. Layer and pooling-window ablations show that value prediction is not confined to a single extraction setting. Extraction ablations; probe architectures.

Scope. The experiments use fixed compute budgets; behavior over substantially longer training horizons remains untested. The estimator predicts sequence-level value. Token-level credit assignment, non-verifiable rewards, and long-horizon agentic tasks are future directions. Limitations.
